# PRODUCT PRINCIPLES

> This document is for the KWEST team.

It guides product decisions, feature prioritization and user experience.
It is not intended for public publication.

---

# KWEST Product Principles

## 1. Community Before Commerce
The needs of residents come first. Revenue should grow because we create value for the community, never at its expense.

---

## 2. Authenticity Over Perfection
Use real businesses, real people, real stories and real places. Progress matters more than polish, but authenticity is never negotiable.

---

## 3. Trust Is Earned, Never Assumed
Every listing, story and update should increase the community's confidence in KWEST.

---

## 4. Solve Real Neighbourhood Problems
Every feature must solve a genuine challenge faced by residents, visitors or local businesses.

---

## 5. Celebrate Local Business While Serving the Whole Community
Businesses are an important part of the neighbourhood, but KWEST exists for everyone.

---

## 6. Simplicity Wins
Simple, intuitive experiences are better than feature-heavy complexity.

---

## 7. Real Stories. Real People. Real Places.
Community content should reflect authentic life in Kahawa West, not manufactured engagement.

---

## 8. Mobile First
Design for the device most residents use every day. Desktop is important, but mobile is the priority.

---

## 9. Build Once, Scale Everywhere
Every architectural decision should make it easier for another neighbourhood to adopt the KWEST model.

---

## 10. Strengthen the Community
Before building any feature, ask:

> Does this make Kahawa West a stronger community?

If the answer is no, rethink it.

## 11. Own the Destination

Social media is for discovery.

KWEST is where trusted information lives.

Every important piece of content should have its permanent home on KWEST.

---

# Decision Filter

Before approving any new feature, answer these five questions:

- Does it help residents?
- Does it strengthen trust?
- Is it simple to understand?
- Will it still make sense in five years?
- Can another neighbourhood benefit from this model?

If most answers are "No", the feature needs to be redesigned or rejected.
